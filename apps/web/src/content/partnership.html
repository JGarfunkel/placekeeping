For preserves, gardens, and campuses

Placekeeping gives a land manager a living record of their place: photographs of chosen spots, taken over time, gathered in one public place and shared with the people who visit.

A place changes season by season and year by year, and most of that change goes unrecorded. Placekeeping keeps the record in the open, tied to the exact spot, so the change stays visible to you and to anyone who cares about the place.

What exists today

The systems that track land were built for other questions. Accession databases record specimens. Protection databases record status and boundaries. Neither one shows the visible life of a place over time.

For that, the tools a manager reaches for now are a custom build on the website or public photo sharing sites. A custom build is costly to make and to maintain. Public photo sharing sites sort photos by date and by account, not by place, so the pictures of a single spot scatter and its change cannot be followed. None of them keeps the record by spot, and that is the piece Placekeeping adds.

What a partner gets now

A record. You define the spots worth watching. Visitors and staff photograph them over time. Each spot has its own page, indexed by location, where observations gather. The record is public, easy to share, and yours to point people toward.

Help defining the spots. Choosing what to watch is its own skill. We work through it with you: which spots reward a return visit, which show change most clearly, which carry the story of the place. You hold the final say on what appears.

Who it is for

Placekeeping is built for land that is open to the public and depends on public engagement: nature preserves, public gardens, land trusts, and nature centers. It fits equally a publicly accessible campus where the grounds are as much a part of the visit as the buildings, such as an arts center or a cultural estate. The same approach extends to any kept land where people gather and someone tends the ground, including school grounds, corporate campuses, and the grounds of a recreational club.
